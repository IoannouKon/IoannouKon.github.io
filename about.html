01 / How I got here

A path from circuits to computing systems.

At NTUA, I discovered that the most interesting part of engineering for me was the space between an algorithm and the machine that executes it. Questions about latency, power, memory, and data movement drew me toward digital design and computer architecture.

My diploma thesis became LUMIXED, a custom LUT-based matrix multiplication accelerator. I built the design in Chisel, integrated it with Rocket/Chipyard, and learned a lot by measuring it, debugging it, and seeing how architectural choices affect real hardware resources.

“I like work where I can understand the whole system — from the idea on paper to the signals on a waveform.”
02 / Projects & experiments

A personal hardware workbench.

My personal projects explore RISC-V, accelerator datapaths, memory structures, FPGA experiments, and ideas that might make machine learning inference more efficient.

I also enjoy contributing improvements to open-source projects such as SNAX, sharing what I learn, and building on the work of the hardware community.
