How I got here.

I studied Electrical and Computer Engineering at NTUA, where I became increasingly interested in digital hardware, computer architecture, and the relationship between algorithmic choices and physical computing resources.

For my diploma thesis, I designed LUMIXED, a mixed-precision LUT-based accelerator built in Chisel and integrated into a RISC-V system. Working across modeling, RTL, simulation, verification, and hardware evaluation made me want to explore more specialized computing architectures.

What excites me.

I especially enjoy the design questions that don't have one obvious answer: whether to trade logic for memory, how to reuse data effectively, how to schedule transfers around computation, and how numerical precision changes hardware costs.

Computer architecture

RISC-V systems, custom accelerators, datapaths, scheduling, and performance trade-offs.

AI hardware

Mixed-precision computation, quantization, and efficient neural-network inference.

RTL & FPGA

Chisel, digital design, verification, simulation, and implementation workflows.

Open source

Learning from existing designs, sharing reproducible work, and contributing improvements.

Where theory meets practice.

Professionally, I work in space on-board software engineering, with experience in embedded software, testing, and verification. This has reinforced an appreciation for robust engineering practices, traceability, and systems where correctness matters.

My independent hardware work complements that experience by letting me explore computational architectures in depth, from first-principles design to implementation and evaluation.
